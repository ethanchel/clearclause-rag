Clause 1 · The problem

We sign things we never read

Leases, terms of service, insurance policies. The answers to practical questions are in there, but the language is dense and nobody has time.

  • A plain chatbot is not the fix: it answers legal questions with confidence, right or wrong For legal content, a wrong answer that sounds right is worse than no answer
  • What we actually need: answers grounded in the document itself, that can be verified If the answer is not in the document, the system should say so
Clause 2 · The product

A RAG assistant with receipts

  • Chat about a document in natural language, with conversation history
  • Every answer cites its source document and page, with the passage shown
  • Document selector: lease, terms of service, or insurance, or search all three
  • Built-in "RAG vs LLM-only" comparison tab and a live evaluation dashboard
  • One-command launch (./run.sh), deploy-ready for Hugging Face Spaces

The pipeline

GenerationQwen2.5-7B via HF API, automatic local 1.5B fallback

Chunk metadata (file, page) travels through the whole pipeline. That is what makes the citations exact.

Clause 4 · Retrieval decisions

Three choices that mattered

  • Chunking on sentence boundaries, not character counts Half sentences make noisy embeddings and unreadable citations
  • Cosine similarity (normalized vectors + inner product index) The metric MiniLM was trained for; scores become comparable across queries
  • A minimum relevance threshold of 0.25 FAISS always returns the nearest neighbors. The threshold turns "nearest" into "near enough", so off-topic questions get an honest "nothing found"

Clause 6 · Evaluation method

Same model, with and without retrieval

Each question is answered twice: once by the full pipeline, once by the same LLM with no documents. If RAG does not beat that baseline, the pipeline is decoration.

  • ROUGE-1 F1 against the expected answer, for both versions
  • Document hit rate: did retrieval find the right document?
  • Page hit rate: did it find the exact expected page? This is the metric that validates the citation feature specifically

Clause 8 · Bonus experiment

LoRA: style is cheap, facts are not

  • Fine-tuned Qwen2.5-0.5B on the legal Q&A pairs with LoRA adapters 540k trainable parameters, 0.11% of the model. Seconds on a laptop
  • On held-out questions the model picked up the target style: short, direct, definite
  • But it invented facts with confidence: "30 days" where the document says 60 Expected with 18 training pairs, and exactly the failure mode RAG exists to prevent
  • Takeaway: fine-tuning shapes how a model answers, retrieval grounds what it answers
Clause 9 · Problems along the way

What production taught me

  • My planned generation model (Mistral-7B) was retired from the API mid-project Probed the live catalog, switched to Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct
  • Free API credits ran out during evaluation Built an automatic local fallback model. The outage became a feature: the demo now works offline
  • faiss-cpu stopped shipping Intel Mac binaries Fixed with a platform-specific version pin in requirements.txt
  • Hugging Face made Gradio Spaces hosting PRO-only in 2026 Repo is deploy-ready and documented; deployment is one subscription away
Clause 10 · Limits and next steps

What I would do with more time

  • Bigger corpus: ingestion already scans a folder, evaluation would get more meaningful
  • Better correctness metric: LLM-as-judge or human grading instead of ROUGE
  • Page ranges per chunk, to fix the three page-boundary misses
  • OCR for scanned PDFs, multilingual embeddings for French documents
